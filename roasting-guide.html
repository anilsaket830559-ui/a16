The Roasting Guide

Temperature, time, resting and browning — and why a thermometer ends the argument.

Roasting is the most forgiving way to cook and the easiest to get wrong, because almost everyone works from minutes per kilogram alone. Weight is a starting point. Internal temperature is the answer.

Start with the oven

Ovens lie. A dial set to 200°C commonly runs anywhere between 180 and 220, and the temperature swings as the element cycles. An oven thermometer costs very little and will explain years of inconsistent results. If you are using a fan oven, drop conventional recipe temperatures by about 20°C.

Temperatures that matter

MeatOvenTarget centreRest
Whole chicken200°C74°C in the thigh15–20 min
Turkey crown180°C74°C25–30 min
Pork loin180°C63°C10–15 min
Leg of lamb (pink)180°C60°C20 min
Beef joint (medium rare)200°C then 160°C55°C20–30 min
Whole fish200°C63°C5 min

Pull the meat out a few degrees below target, because carry-over cooking continues to raise the centre while it rests. A large joint can climb five degrees after it leaves the oven; a chicken rather less.

Why resting works

Heat drives moisture towards the centre of a piece of meat and tightens the muscle fibres. Resting lets both relax, so the juice stays in the meat rather than flooding the board when you carve. Cover loosely with foil, somewhere warm but not hot. A big joint will still be hot half an hour later.

Browning

Colour is flavour, and it needs a dry surface and real heat. Pat the skin dry, salt it an hour ahead if you can, and start hot. Crowding is the usual enemy: a tray packed edge to edge releases steam faster than it escapes, and nothing browns. Two trays beat one every time.

Vegetables in the same oven

Cut everything to a similar size so it cooks at the same rate. Dense roots need 40 to 50 minutes; softer vegetables want 25 to 30. Toss in oil and salt but hold back anything sweet, such as honey or balsamic, until the last ten minutes, or it burns before the inside is done.

Gravy from the tray

Lift the meat out to rest. Pour off most of the fat, leaving the dark sticky residue. Put the tray over a low flame, add a spoon of flour and cook it out for a minute, then add stock a little at a time, scraping as you go. Simmer until it coats a spoon. Season at the end, because reduction concentrates the salt that was already there.

Timing it all

Use the Roast Timer for the joint, then build the rest of the evening around it with the Dinner Timeline. The resting window is not dead time; it is when the gravy, the greens and the warm plates happen.

Food safety, briefly

Poultry and minced meat must be cooked through: 74°C for poultry, 71°C for mince. Whole cuts of beef and lamb can be served pink because the inside is sterile until it is cut. Cool leftovers quickly, refrigerate within two hours and reheat once, until piping hot.
